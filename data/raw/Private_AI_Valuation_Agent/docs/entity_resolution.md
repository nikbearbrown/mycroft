# Entity resolution — the deterministic floor, and what an LLM did not add

**Status:** sections 1–9 are complete and measured (Weeks 4–5). Section 10 is reserved for
Week 6, the human review queue, and is empty because the work has not been done.

**The result in one line:** a deterministic matcher resolves 322 labelled issuer strings at
precision 0.9959 / recall 1.0000, and adding a local 8B model to the resolution path
**costs 5.1 points of precision**. The matcher ships; the model does not. See §9.

**Golden set:** `tests/fixtures/golden_set_v1.json` v1.0.0 · 322 labelled issuer strings
covering 7,276 holdings
**Metrics:** `docs/_matcher_metrics.json` (§7) and `docs/_adjudication_metrics.json` (§9),
regenerated by `python -m scripts.score_matcher` and
`python -m scripts.run_adjudication --score`

**Human attestation:** **the adjudications only** — Om Mali, 2026-08-22. Scope and what was

## 1. The problem, sized

N-PORT gives every filer a free-text `ISSUER_NAME` and a free-text `ISSUER_TITLE`. Nobody
validates either. The result, measured across the fourteen quarters in
`data/parquet/*/private_holdings.parquet`:

All name counts here are **case-insensitive**, because `ANTHROPIC PBC` and `Anthropic PBC` are
one spelling in two casings, not two spellings. The case-sensitive figures are given alongside
where they differ, since the earlier drafts of this document mixed the two conventions and
produced two different numbers for the same quantity.

| | |
|---|---|
| Holdings in the private layer | 22,041,937 |
| Distinct issuer names | 3,204,853 (3,206,764 case-sensitive) |
| Distinct normalised tokens across those names | 3,472,160 |
| Holdings the frozen Week 2 patterns select | 5,806 |
| Distinct names among those 5,806 | 154 (166 case-sensitive) |
| — of which the 7 universe v1 companies | **128** |
| — of which watchlisted companies | 24 |
| — of which the deliberate `%COHERE%` canary | 2 |
| Distinct (name, title) pairs among those 5,806 | 231 |

Databricks alone arrives under **51 distinct issuer names and 69 distinct issuer titles**.
Anthropic arrives under 10 names and 22 titles. That is the whole of the problem: **7 companies
wearing 128 names**, inside a corpus of 3.2 million.

The corpus is also almost entirely hapax. 79% of normalised tokens appear in exactly one
distinct name, and the document-frequency distribution has a median of 1 and a 99th
percentile of 6. This matters because it kills the obvious idea first: an inverse-document-
frequency weighting cannot tell a distinctive token from a generic one here, because on this
corpus almost every token is distinctive. That measurement is why §4's gate is a coverage
rule and not an IDF score.

## 2. Normalisation

`src/resolve/normalize.py` reduces a name to three views: `core` (single-spaced, corporate
forms and class vocabulary removed), `dense` (`core` with every separator deleted), and
`tokens`. Three of its rules were forced by specific rows rather than chosen on principle.

**A dot inside an alphabetic run is deleted, not spaced.** `X.AI` and `xAI` are one company.
The frozen pattern `%X.AI%` misses Fidelity's spelling `XAI CORP` — **85 holdings**, the
largest single recall gap in the shipped pattern set, and Fidelity is X.AI's largest holder.
`_dedot` closes it. A dot followed by a space stays a boundary, so `INC. SERIES` does not
fuse.

**The rule needed a companion, and finding out why cost a wrong label.** De-dotting fuses a
domain suffix into the stem: `OPENAIR.COM` became the single token `OPENAIRCOM`, which scores
75 against the alias `OPENAI` and is rejected. That looked like a lucky escape — right up
until the rows were read. `OpenAir.com` is BlackRock's and New York Life's spelling of
**OpenAI**, titled `OpenAir.com, Series C` and priced at 687.6869 (§6). The de-dot rule was
silently costing five real holdings.

So a dot-TLD is now stripped *before* de-dotting: `.COM`, `.NET`, `.ORG`, `.IO`. It is
punctuation with a domain attached, not identity, and none of the universe companies is named
after one — the corpus's other dot-TLD issuers are Amazon.com, Businessolver.com, Mercor.io
and their kind, none of which the strip causes the matcher to claim. **`.AI` is deliberately
excluded and must stay excluded**, because `X.AI` is a company's entire identity and stripping
it would undo the 85-holding recall fix above. The two rules pull in opposite directions on
the same character, and the only thing keeping them straight is that the list is explicit.

`OPENAIR` now scores **0.9231** against `OPENAI` — a one-character difference, structurally
the same as `ANTHROPICS`/`ANTHROPIC` at 0.947. It clears the gate's per-token minimum of 92 by
0.31, which is thinner than any margin here ought to be; raising `TOKEN_SIM` to 93 would lose
it again.

**A `dense` key is kept alongside `core`.** BlackRock writes `Open AI Group PBC` with a
space; everyone else writes `OpenAI`. That is a separator disagreement, not a spelling
difference, and no token-based scorer can see it. Comparing on `dense` can.

**Series designators are never folded.** `WORLD LABS SER C PC PP` and
`WORLD LABS SER C PRIME PC PP` are two securities, held by the same ten funds, priced at
**$314.76–315.31** and **$337.66–338.23** respectively on the same period ends. Anything
that collapses `C PRIME` into `C` merges two real price series and reappears later as
dispersion that is not there. `PRIME` is therefore classified as class vocabulary — it must
leave the name key and survive in the class designator.

One entry in the stop-word list is worth naming because it looks wrong: **`LABS` is not
stripped.** It reads like a generic descriptor and is not one. `World Labs` is the whole of
that company's identity, and removing it leaves the bare word `WORLD`, which fuzzy-matches a
dozen unrelated issuers. `TECHNOLOGIES`, `SYSTEMS`, `INDUSTRIES` and `GROUP` *are* stripped,
because `Space Exploration`, `Cerebras`, `Anduril` and `OpenAI` survive without them.

`PCB` is also in the corporate-form list, deliberately. Two filers write `OPENAI GROUP PCB`,
transposing PBC. Treating the transposition as a corporate form makes it disappear instead
of having to be enumerated.

## 3. Share-class grammar

The parser answers two questions: which round or class is this, and **is the price a share
price at all**. The second is the one that can corrupt a panel silently.

| Signal | Meaning | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| `PC` | preferred convertible | Of 1,987 universe holdings whose title contains ` PC `, **all 1,987** are tagged `EP` by their filer and none is tagged `EC` or `OTHER`. The most reliable preferred signal in the corpus. |
| `SER E P` | preferred, truncated | SpaceX. These rows price in the 810–5,265 band while the same filer's common sits at 70–527. |
| bare `SERIES <letter>` | preferred | A private company does not issue "Series G common". |
| `CLASS <letter>` | common | Price-confirmed for SpaceX: Class A and Class C sit in the 77–527 common band. |
| `CVT INT RIGHTS`, `RTS 1:1` | **not a share** | See below. |
| `EV UNITS`, `PROFIT PARTICIPATION UNITS` | **not a share** | Balance is units of a vehicle. |
| `nn% ... and nn% ...` | **blended classes** | One holding line spanning two classes; the price belongs to neither. |

### The instrument that is not a share

`OPEN AI GLOBAL LLC CONVERTIBLE INTEREST RT PP` carries `BALANCE` exactly equal to
`CURRENCY_VALUE` — 2,486,039 shares and $2,486,039 — so it prices at exactly **$1.00**. It
is a dollar-denominated convertible right, not a share. **231 holdings** across **seven
title spellings** carry a price that is not a share price, and every one of them is OpenAI:
convertible interest rights, `RTS 1:1 @ USD`, profit-participation units, `EV UNITS`, and
the typo `OPENAI LLV CVT INT RIGHTS PP`. A price of $1.00 looks like a price.

This is why `price_basis` is a first-class label and not a derived convenience: OpenAI's
common-stock price range in the universe layer runs from $1.00 to $769.43, and the bottom of
that range is an artefact.

### Measured against a field the parser never reads

`ASSET_CAT` is set by the filer in a separate field, so it is an independent check.

| | |
|---|---|
| Holdings where both the grammar and `ASSET_CAT` commit to common-or-preferred | 4,045 |
| Agree | 3,619 (**89.47%**) |
| Disagree | 426 |

The disagreements are not noise, and they do not exonerate the grammar automatically — so
both directions were read:

- **414 holdings: the filer says `EC` (common), the grammar says preferred.** These are rows
  like `Databricks, Inc., Series G` and `Anduril Industries, Inc., Series F` tagged as common
  stock, and `SPACE EXPLORATION TECH SER E P` tagged `EC` by Neuberger Berman while priced in
  the preferred band. Databricks Series G is preferred stock. **The grammar is right and the
  filers are mis-tagging**, and the mis-tagging is concentrated: Franklin, BlackRock, Alger,
  New York Life and Legg Mason account for most of it.
- **12 holdings: the filer says `EP` (preferred), the grammar says common.** All of them are
  Baron's `X.AI HOLDINGS CORP., CLASS B` and `CLASS C`. Here the filer is probably right —
  for xAI, "Class B/C" most likely denotes the preferred rounds — which means the rule
  `CLASS <letter>` → common is a real approximation, kept because it is price-confirmed for
  SpaceX's 100-plus common holdings. **It changes no mark:** X.AI's `CLASS`-titled rows
  report exactly the same price as its other rows in every overlapping period ($36.56,
  $65.19, $75.46). It is recorded, not fixed. See §7.

### What a class parse is actually for

Precision on a class label is not the goal; a coherent price series is. So: within one
(company, share class, period end), do the filers agree?

| | |
|---|---|
| Cells | 1,010 |
| Cells with two or more filers | 694 |
| Agree within 0.5% | 474 (**68.3%**) |
| Cells spanning a near-integer ratio | 14 |

All 14 near-integer cells are SpaceX, all at exactly **10.00×**, and all with a parsed class
of `UNKNOWN` — titles like `SPACE EXPLORATION TECH CORP` that carry no class information at
all. That is the precise diagnosis of the Week 2 10× finding: **when the title omits the
class, the 10×-priced preferred and the common collapse into the same cell.** The remaining
206 disagreeing cells are single-digit-percent spreads, which is cross-manager dispersion —
the project's actual subject — and not a parsing failure.

## 4. The matcher

Four paths, tried in order, each more speculative than the last. The first to fire wins and
records how, so every resolved holding traces to the rule that resolved it.

| Path | Condition | Score |
|---|---|---|
| `lei` | `ISSUER_LEI` is one of 7 verified issuer LEIs | 1.00 |
| `alias` | the normalised `core` or `dense` equals a known alias | 1.00 |
| `spv` | the wrapper's parenthetical or `dba` clause resolves via the above | ≤ 0.95 |
| `fuzzy` | the coverage gate passes | < 1.00 |

**The LEI path is not decoration.** 2,951 of the 5,806 universe holdings — **50.8%** — carry
one of 7 LEIs, each verified to map to exactly one company and to appear on no other issuer's
rows. `AESTAS LLC dba OPENAI LLC EV UNITS PP` carries OpenAI's LEI, so the SPV inherits its
underlying's registered identifier. A registered identifier is identity; a name is a
heuristic; the ordering follows.

**The alias table holds 14 entries for 11 companies** — what each company calls itself, plus
its corporate forms. It deliberately contains none of the 166 observed filer spellings. An
alias table stuffed with observed spellings would score perfectly on the golden set and
predict nothing about next quarter, and a test fails if the table grows past 16.

### Why the fuzzy path needs a gate and not a threshold

`token_set_ratio` scores a subset as a perfect match. Measured on real corpus names:

```
token_set_ratio('FIGURE',              'FIGURE AI')  = 100   wrong company
token_set_ratio('OPEN BAY AUTOS AI',   'OPEN AI')    = 100   wrong company
token_set_ratio('ANDURIL ENGINEERING', 'ANDURIL')    = 100   right company
```

One true positive and two false positives, all three at the top of the range. No cut-off on
that scorer can separate them, so the scorer cannot be the thing that decides.

The gate is a necessary condition evaluated before any score: **every token of the alias must
be matched by some token of the query at 92 or better.** It rejects `FIGURE`, because nothing
in it accounts for `AI`. It admits `ANDURIL ENGINEERING LLC`, which is a true Anduril
position (§5). `TOKEN_SIM` is 92 rather than 90 for one measured reason: `SPACE` scores 90.9
against the alias `SPACEX`, and `RELATIVITY SPACE INC` — 415 holdings of a different rocket
company — is not SpaceX.

Given the gate, the score expresses only how much of the query the alias accounts for:
`coverage × (1 − 0.10 × unexplained_tokens)`, where a token is explained if it matched an
alias token or belongs to a generic fund-structure vocabulary. Sponsor names are deliberately
*not* in that vocabulary, so an SPV whose sponsor cannot be accounted for scores below a
direct holding — which is the right ordering of doubt.

**Blocking** is a trigram inverted index over the alias keys. With 11 companies it saves
nothing, and it is written anyway because the production input is 3.2 million distinct names.
It is a recall-preserving filter and nothing more: `PUBLIC JOINT STOCK COMPANY PHOSAGRO`
survives blocking on the trigram `GRO` it shares with GROQ, and is rejected one step later.

## 5. The golden set

**322 labelled issuer strings covering 7,276 holdings.** `plan.md` asked for 200–300 labelled
*holdings*; the unit here is the distinct (name, title) pair instead, because the 5,806
universe holdings contain only 231 distinct pairs and a random sample of 250 holdings would
be ~95% repeats of `DATABRICKS INC`. Labelling the distinct strings covers every spelling in
the corpus, and each entry carries its holding count so precision and recall can be reported
both per-string (macro) and per-holding (micro). The count came out above the plan's band and
was left there rather than tuning a sampling parameter to hit a round number.

### Sampling frame — `scripts/build_golden_candidates.py`

| Stratum | Strings | What it decides |
|---|---|---|
| S1 universe | 237 | Precision. A wrong one is a false positive already in the shipped data. |
| S2 near-miss | 82 | Recall. Names in the 22M-row private layer scoring ≥ 82 against a company's own name that the frozen patterns do **not** select. |
| S3 hard cases | 81 | The five `plan.md` names, pinned by literal. |

The near-miss sweep returns 347 unselected names — too many, and 74 of them are one filer's
internal security codes (`XAIE-LMBK.AF` and 39 siblings), where labelling forty measures
nothing. Two rules cut it down, and the second is the important one:

- at most 4 names per near-miss token, taken by holding volume; and
- **unconditionally, every near-miss name matcher v1 claims.** No claim the matcher makes can
  escape being judged, which is what makes the precision figure a property of the matcher
  rather than of how the sample was drawn.

All five `plan.md` hard cases are present and a test fails if any drops out:
`S3_anthropic_variants` (22), `S3_spacex_10x` (39), `S3_ark_ec_tagged_preferred` (13),
`S3_world_labs_c_prime` (4), `S3_opaque_spv` (3). The opaque Fidelity SPVs reach the sample
only by being pinned: they resemble nothing, which is exactly their point.

### Labels, and what decided them

Each label records the evidence class that assigned it.

| Class | Strings | Holdings | Basis |
|---|---|---|---|
| `E1_lei` | 108 | 4,449 | a verified registered issuer LEI on the row |
| `E2_self_name` | 100 | 1,349 | the name contains the company's own name as exact contiguous tokens |
| `E4_spv_disclosure` | 23 | 62 | the wrapper's own name discloses its underlying |
| `E6_adjudicated` | 10 | 76 | an explicit written decision, §6 |
| `E5_opaque` | 3 | 30 | a wrapper disclosing nothing → `UNKNOWN` |
| `E0_none` | 78 | 1,310 | nothing fires → `NOT_IN_UNIVERSE` |

The **price anchor** (`E3`) decided no label on its own. It is the per-period consensus price
computed *only* from rows with a verified LEI or the company's exact own name — never from
the fuzzy matcher, so it can be used to judge it. It was the deciding evidence inside 6 of
the 10 adjudications, and elsewhere it corroborated labels the LEI or the name had already
settled. Two bugs had to be fixed before it could be trusted: Databricks' term loans are
tagged `LON` with balance equal to value, so they price at exactly 1.00 and made every
unrelated issuer priced at 1.00 "confirm" as Databricks; and one Databricks row prices at
**−0.005**, which made a ratio test pass for anything at all because the denominator was
negative. An anchor is only as good as its instrument filter.

### On circularity

`E2` is the same reading the matcher performs. Entries labelled by it test whether
normalisation survives a filer's spelling — not whether the matcher can discover an identity.
Counting them as evidence of discrimination would flatter the matcher, so every metric below
is also reported on a **hard subset**: the 144 strings (4,617 holdings) whose label rests on
`E1`, `E4`, `E5` or `E6`. **Read the hard subset first.**

`UNKNOWN` entries are excluded from precision and recall entirely and counted separately.
Scoring them either way would be inventing a fact.

### The withdrawal, in full

The `OPENAIR.COM` entry was originally labelled `NOT_IN_UNIVERSE` with this reason: *its price
coincides with an OpenAI anchor in exactly one period, and a single-period coincidence between
unrelated issuers is ordinary.* **That statement was false.** There are five holdings across
**two** period ends, all at 687.6869, and the title is `OpenAir.com, Series C` — OpenAI's
Series C is precisely the round marked at 687.6869, reported to the same four decimals by
eight holdings across six registrants on 2026-03-31 whose identity is not in dispute — one
carrying OpenAI's registered issuer identifier, the other seven naming OpenAI outright.
Every issuer priced at 687.69 in those two periods is an OpenAI spelling. The filers are BlackRock (three holdings)
and New York Life (two).

The label is now `OpenAI Group PBC`, on the same reasoning that settles `ANTHROPICS TECHNOLOGY
LTD.` and `OPENAI FOUNDATION`: a security-master collision with a similarly-named company,
caught by price. The approval was obtained on a bad statement of the evidence, so it is
recorded as withdrawn rather than carried over — an attestation is worth what its reasoning is
worth.

Two things follow, and both are improvements:

- **Matcher v1 was wrong about this row too**, and worse than the frozen patterns were: the
  patterns select it (via `%OPENAI%` matching `OPENAIR`) and v1 rejected it. That is a
  five-holding false negative, fixed by the dot-TLD strip in §2. It is the clearest case in
  this project so far of the golden set doing the job it exists for — the fix was driven by
  this entry, which is stated plainly in §7 rather than presented as an independent result.
- **`OPEN BAY AUTOS AI INC.` remains the one confirmed negative** among the adjudications, and
  it is now the only remaining false positive anywhere in §7.

**`ANTHROPICS TECHNOLOGY LTD.` → Anthropic PBC.** Anthropics Technology Ltd is a real British
software company and this is not it. BlackRock files three holdings under that name, titled
`Series G`, priced at **259.13640004** and **259.13641638** on 2026-03-31 — the period end on
which fourteen LEI-confirmed holdings across ten registrants price Anthropic Series G at
**259.1364**. Agreement to ten significant figures is not a coincidence.
`INVESTMENT_COUNTRY` is `GB`, inherited from the wrong entity in BlackRock's security
master. This is the single label most worth a second
human look, and it is the one the matcher gets right by fuzzy score (0.947) rather than by
anything principled.

**`ANDURIL ENGINEERING LLC` and `ANDURIL INVESTORS LLC` → Anduril Industries.** BlackRock
holding vehicles, not separate issuers. Prices match the Anduril consensus exactly across
nine period ends — 21.7366, 23.16, 40.88, 68.95 — including the long flat stretch at 40.88
that no unrelated issuer would reproduce to four decimals.

**`OPENAI FOUNDATION` → OpenAI Group PBC.** The Foundation is the non-profit parent and is
not an issuer of equity, so on its face this is the wrong entity. Franklin (twice) and Nuveen
report it at 687.69 and 687.6869 — the OpenAI Group PBC consensus to the cent on both period
ends. Recorded explicitly because the two entities are genuinely distinct.

**`ANTHROPIC, INC.` → Anthropic PBC.** Anthropic is a public benefit corporation, not an Inc.
ARK Venture Fund has filed it this way for thirteen consecutive period ends with a price
series that tracks Anthropic throughout. Recorded because the discrepancy was examined, not
because the mechanical rule failed — `E2` reaches the same answer.

**`SPACE EXPLORATION TECHNOLOGICS` → SpaceX.** A typo for `TECHNOLOGIES`. Ten holdings,
priced in the 970–5,265.90 preferred band.

**`XAI CORP.` → X.AI Corp.** Its price coincides with an *Anduril* anchor, because Anduril's
mark sat flat at 40.88 for seven consecutive period ends and X.AI passed through that price.
The name decides this one; the price would have got it wrong. This is why `E3` requires two
or more periods and still ranks below the name.

**`OPENAIR.COM` → OpenAI Group PBC. Corrected 2026-08-22** — see "The withdrawal, in full"
above. Five holdings titled `OpenAir.com, Series C` at 687.6869, filed by BlackRock and New
York Life, on the two period ends where OpenAI's Series C consensus is 687.6869.

**`OPEN BAY AUTOS AI INC.` → NOT_IN_UNIVERSE.** A used-car marketplace. §7.

Two systems, same labels. **A** is the frozen `%ANTHROPIC%`-style pattern set that produced

**Read the hard-subset rows honestly: baseline A has perfect precision there and matcher v1
does not.** v1 claims `OPEN BAY AUTOS AI INC.` and the frozen patterns do not. v1's case is
recall — 85 holdings of `XAI CORP` that the patterns miss entirely — and dressing it up as a
precision win would be the easy lie here. A test asserts this ordering so it cannot quietly
disappear.

Per-company micro precision and recall for B are 1.000 for every company except OpenAI
(0.9979 precision, one false-positive holding).

**Baseline A's 7 errors, all of them named:** 2 false positives —
`COHERE TECHNOLOGIES, INC. PREFERRED SERIES D-1` and `D-2`, the deliberate `%COHERE%` canary
in `universe.py`, which is a wireless company and not Cohere the model company. 5 false
negatives — every spelling of `XAI CORP`, **85 holdings**.

**Matcher v1's 1 error:** `OPEN BAY AUTOS AI INC.`, one holding.

So this week's work removes 85 missed holdings and 28 wrongly-included ones, and introduces
one wrongly-included one. That is the whole of the case for it.

**One of these numbers is not independent, and it should be said outright.** Matcher v1's
recall is 1.0000 only *after* the dot-TLD strip described in §2, and that fix was written
because this golden set exposed the `OPENAIR.COM` miss. Measured before the fix — which is how
it stood when the error was found — v1 was macro P 0.9958 / **R 0.9958**, with a five-holding
false negative. The golden set drove a real improvement, which is what it is for; it did not
independently validate that improvement, and cannot. Week 5's adjudication layer is the next
thing measured against these labels and inherits the same caveat.

| auto-accept | ≥ 0.90 | resolve without review | 137 of 141 strings; 0 false positives |
| **review** | 0.80 – 0.90 | route to a human | 4 strings per full re-resolution |
| reject | < 0.80 | leave unresolved | — |

The review band exists because the tie above cannot be broken by a number, and it is
deliberately narrow: at a full 14-quarter re-resolution it holds **4 issuer strings**. That
is a bounded amount of human attention for the removal of the only known false positive, and
it is the concrete argument for Week 6's review queue rather than a bigger threshold.

## 8. What was not tested, and what is still open

**Only the adjudications are attested.** 8 strings covering 68 holdings were confirmed. The
other 314 labels are agent-assigned by the
documented procedure in `scripts/label_golden_set.py` and have not been reviewed by anyone.
Every entry carries the evidence that decided it, so the set is auditable row by row, but
under P1 auditability is not attestation. The scope is recorded in the fixture and in §6.

**One attested label was withdrawn within hours of being given, and that is the strongest
argument in this document for not trusting the rest.** `OPENAIR.COM` was labelled, reasoned
about wrongly, approved on that wrong reasoning, and then reversed on the rows. The procedure
that produced the other 314 labels is the same procedure that produced that one. The `E1` LEI
labels rest on registered identifiers and are hard to get wrong; the `E0` negatives are where
another `OPENAIR.COM` would hide, because nothing fires on them and nothing draws attention to
them. **78 strings, 1,310 holdings, unreviewed.**

**The shipped universe layer needs no backfill for this correction, but it does need one for
the canary.** The frozen `%OPENAI%` pattern already selects `OPENAIR.COM`, so its 5 holdings
sit in the universe Parquet and in Postgres `raw_holdings` labelled `OpenAI Group PBC` — which
the correction confirms is right. The `%COHERE%` canary rows, 28 holdings of Cohere
Technologies, *are* wrongly in there and remain so; removing them is a universe-version
change, not a matcher change.

**The metrics are measured on bulk data only.** The live EDGAR path is Week 3 work that has
not been done, so no current-quarter filing has been through this matcher. Bulk data reaches
2026-04-30 at the latest, per the Week 2 filing-lag finding.

**A name that contains a company's name but is a different company will resolve.** The gate's
necessary condition is exactly that, and it is the residual risk by construction — a
hypothetical `ANTHROPIC MINING CORP` would resolve to Anthropic at 0.90 and land inside the
auto-accept band. Nothing in the corpus does this today. This is the specific gap Week 5's
adjudication layer has to be measured against.

**`CLASS <letter>` → common is an approximation.** Baron's 12 X.AI `CLASS B`/`CLASS C`
holdings are tagged `EP` by the filer and are probably preferred. Left as-is because the rule
is price-confirmed for SpaceX's 100-plus common holdings and because it changes no mark, but
it is wrong for at least one company and will need a per-company override before Week 7's
marks panel.

**The dot-TLD strip and the de-dot rule disagree about `.AI` by fiat.** `.COM`, `.NET`,
`.ORG` and `.IO` are stripped and `.AI` is not, purely because `X.AI` is a company and the
others are domains. The list is explicit and tested, but it is a list, not a principle, and a
universe that later includes a `*.ai` issuer will need it revisited. `OPENAIR` clears the
per-token gate at 0.9231 against a minimum of 92 — a 0.31 margin, thinner than anything else
here.

**The 68.3% price-consistency figure is not decomposed.** 14 of the 220 disagreeing cells are
identified as 10× class artefacts. The remaining 206 are assumed to be genuine cross-manager
dispersion and have not been individually examined. Week 8 measures dispersion properly; if
some of those 206 turn out to be parsing failures, the figure moves.

**The frame is reproducible; the labels are re-derivable.** Both are checked, and neither was
true at first. The frame rebuilt to 322 strings on one run and 323 on the next until three
ordering bugs were fixed — DuckDB's `DISTINCT` has no ordering guarantee, `process.extract`
breaks equal scores by input position, and `price_evidence` iterated a set of company names
so `PYTHONHASHSEED` chose which anchor was credited. It now rebuilds byte-identically.
`tests/test_resolve.py::test_the_fixture_still_matches_the_frame_it_was_built_from`
re-derives every label from the committed frame, but it does **not** re-run the
3.2-million-name sweep, so drift in the sweep itself would not be caught by the test suite.

**Amended filings are still not adjudicated.** 16 `NPORT-P/A` amendments (71 holdings) sit
beside their originals, carried forward from Week 2. Unchanged by this week's work and still
required before Week 7.

**No figure was produced for this week.** The metrics live in a table and a JSON file.

## 9. LLM adjudication — measured, and not adopted

**Verdict: keep the deterministic matcher.** An 8B local model, given the same evidence, costs
**5.1 points of precision** when it is allowed to name a company. `plan.md` asked for this
measurement and told us what to do with it — *"if there is no lift, keep the deterministic
matcher and say so"* — so this section says so, and shows the working.

| | |
|---|---|
| Model | `llama3.1:8b` · 8.0B params · Q4_K_M · digest `46e0c10c039e0191` |
| Runtime | Ollama 0.33.1, local, RTX 4070 Laptop 8 GB — 5.3 GB resident, 100% on GPU |
| Decoding | `temperature 0`, `seed 7`, JSON-Schema-constrained |
| Run | 322 calls, 1,042 s wall, **0 failures** |
| Inputs | issuer name, issuer title, filer, candidate list — no price, no label |
| Artifacts | `docs/_adjudication_results.json` (every raw reply), `docs/_adjudication_metrics.json` |

The prompt never contains the answer, and a test checks that for all 322 entries. Price is
withheld deliberately: price is what settled the hard labels in §6, and the deterministic
matcher does not get it either, so handing it to the model would measure a different system.

### 9.1 The policies

Four ways to combine a model with a matcher, plus the matcher alone.

| System | The model may… |
|---|---|
| **B** `matcher_v1` | — nothing. Week 4's deterministic baseline. |
| **C** `v2_band` | answer where the matcher scored 0.80–0.90 or resolved nothing |
| **D** `v2_all` | overrule anything short of an exact LEI or alias match |
| **E** `llm_only` | decide alone; the matcher is ignored |
| **F** `v2_veto` | **only withdraw** a weak claim, never create one — see §9.4 |

An LEI match is never put to a vote under any policy. A registered identifier is identity, and
a model that can overrule it is not adding judgment, it is adding noise to the one part of the
pipeline that was never in doubt.

### 9.2 Results — 322 labelled strings, macro

| System | Precision | Recall | F1 | fp | fn | Lift (F1) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **B** matcher v1 | 0.9959 | 1.0000 | 0.9979 | 1 | 0 | — |
| C v2 band | 0.9449 | 1.0000 | 0.9717 | 14 | 0 | **−0.0262** |
| D v2 all | 0.9447 | 0.9958 | 0.9696 | 14 | 1 | **−0.0283** |
| E llm only | 0.9447 | 0.9958 | 0.9696 | 14 | 1 | **−0.0283** |
| F v2 veto | 1.0000 | 1.0000 | 1.0000 | 0 | 0 | +0.0021 |

Per holding, the band policy turns 1 wrongly-included holding into **196**.

**The hard subset lies here, and it is worth saying why.** On the 144-string hard subset every
LLM policy scores 1.0000 precision and looks like a clean win. That subset was defined in Week
4 to exclude labels decided by the name — which excludes the `E0` negatives, and the negatives
are the *only* place the model does damage. A subset chosen to test one system is not
automatically the right lens for the next one. Read the full set for this question.

### 9.3 How it fails: it promotes resemblances

Under the band policy the model changed 15 of the 85 strings it saw. It **fixed 1 and broke
14**, and all fourteen have the same shape — the matcher had resolved nothing, and the model
promoted the row to a company because the name looked a bit like one:

| Holding | Model said | Its stated reason |
|---|---|---|
| `HYPERSCALE DATA INC` (×5) | Scale AI | *"is the parent company of Scale AI, Inc."* |
| `SCALED AGILE, INC.-REVOLVER` (×3) | Scale AI | *"the issuer name and title match the name of Scale AI"* |
| `ABL SPACABL SPACE SYSTEMS…` | SpaceX | *"the issuer name matches the name of a company in the candidate list"* |
| `OPEN SPACE LABS INC` | SpaceX | *"a clear match for SpaceX"* |
| `COHERE TECHNOLOGIES … SERIES D-1` | Cerebras | *"is a preferred series of Cerebras Systems Inc."* |
| `XAI3-FT5O.AF`, `XAIJ-C5DQ.AF` | X.AI | *"the issuer name and title are identical, and match…"* |
| `J7PV-OPEN.AF` | OpenAI | *"match the known OpenAI naming convention"* |

Three of these are worth dwelling on. `HYPERSCALE DATA INC` is *not* Scale AI's parent — that
is an invented corporate fact, and the prompt's first rule forbids exactly that. A term loan to
`SCALED AGILE` is a loan to a different company. And `XAI3-FT5O.AF` is a **Fidelity internal
security code**, one of 74 in the corpus, resolved to X.AI on the strength of three characters.

Two replies contradict themselves outright: the `reason` field reads *"the issuer name and
title do not match any of the candidate companies"* while the `company` field names Scale AI.
The prose was right and the structured field was wrong, in the same response.

### 9.4 Confidence is not usable

The model returned **1.000 confidence on 315 of 322 answers**. It used exactly three values
in the whole run — 1.0 (315 times), 0.95 (4) and 0.0 (3) — so the field carries almost no
information before it is even checked against the labels.

Against the labels it carries none. **Fifteen** answers disagree with their label, and
**twelve of those came back at 0.95 or above**; eight arrived at a flat 1.0, one of them with
a reason saying it did not match anything. Narrowing to the fourteen strings the band policy
actually got wrong that the matcher alone got right: eleven at 0.95 or above, seven at 1.0.
The two denominators are different questions and section 9.2 keeps them apart — fifteen is
the model's own error set, fourteen is the damage the shipping-candidate policy would do.

This matters beyond this week: Week 6's review queue would naturally want to sort by model
confidence, and on this evidence it cannot. A test pins the finding.

*Corrected 2026-08-28.* This section first read "308 of 322" and "nine of the fourteen".
Both were typed by hand and both were wrong. The dot-per-answer figure built for the video
generates its counts straight from the cached replies, and it disagreed with the prose, which
is how the error surfaced. `scripts/run_adjudication.py` now computes a `confidence` block
into `docs/_adjudication_metrics.json`, and the numbers above are read from it — a count no
script produced is a P3 violation whether or not it happens to be right.

### 9.5 The veto policy, and why a perfect score means little here

Every break was a *promotion* (nothing → a company). The single fix was a *demotion*: the
matcher had claimed OpenAI for `OPEN BAY AUTOS AI INC.`, a used-car marketplace, and the model
withdrew it. So the model is a decent skeptic and a poor proposer, and **F** grants it only the
power it demonstrated.

**F** scores 1.0000/1.0000. That number should not be believed as a general result:

- It is consulted on **4 of 322** strings in the whole golden set, and **3 of 231** in the
  shipped universe layer.
- Of those four it vetoed one — correctly — and was right about the other three *by declining
  to act*.
- A perfect score on n=4 describes four rows. It is not evidence of a robust improvement.
- The policy was designed **after** reading the failures, so this golden set motivated it and
  cannot also validate it. Same caveat as Week 4's dot-TLD fix.

And the decisive objection is architectural, not statistical: **those four strings are the same
four that Week 4 already routes to a human review queue.** The veto policy is not saving human
effort, it is pre-empting a review that was already going to happen, on exactly the rows where
the matcher admits doubt. It is retained in the code, measured, and **off by default**.

### 9.6 Throughput, and the cost of a full re-resolution

| | |
|---|---|
| Mean per call | **3.236 s** (median 3.205, slowest 3.669) |
| Throughput | 196.9 tokens/s · 205,214 tokens over the run |
| Cold load | 2.4 s |
| Failures | **0 of 322** — schema-constrained decoding held |

Cost against the **shipped universe layer** (231 distinct name/title pairs, 5,806 holdings):

| Policy | Calls | Wall clock |
|---|---|---|
| E llm only | 231 | 12.5 min |
| C v2 band | 5 | 16 s |
| F v2 veto | 3 | 10 s |

The band policy consults 26.4% of the *golden set* but only 2.2% of the *corpus*, because the
golden set deliberately over-samples near-miss negatives. Cost measured on the golden set
overstates the real bill by an order of magnitude, and quoting the golden-set rate as an
operating cost would be wrong.

Cost is not the reason to decline. Twelve minutes for a full re-resolution is affordable. The
reason is that it buys negative accuracy.

### 9.7 What this does not show

- **One model, one size, one prompt.** `llama3.1:8b` at Q4_K_M. A 70B model, or a
  reasoning-tuned one, may well clear the bar; none was tried, because the plan specifies an
  8B-class local model and that is what was measured.
- **No prompt iteration.** One prompt, written once, run once. Iterating against these labels
  would fit the prompt to the test set, and the honest version of that experiment needs a
  held-out split the golden set is too small to give up.
- **Share class was not scored.** The model returned a non-UNKNOWN class on 215 of 322 replies,
  but the fixture's own class labels are parser output (§5), so scoring one against the other
  would measure agreement between two guesses.
- **The 78 `E0` negatives are still unattested** by a human, and they are precisely where the
  model failed. If some of those labels are wrong, the direction of this result could move —
  though it would have to move a long way to reverse a 5.1-point precision gap.
- **Determinism across versions is unverified.** `temperature 0` and a fixed seed make the run
  repeatable on this machine and this build; Ollama does not guarantee bit-identical output
  across model or runtime upgrades, which is why the digest is recorded.
