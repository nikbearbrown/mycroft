## Open questions - answered
 
**1. Does the roughly flat 2026 Reality Labs loss guidance change your view of the segment as a risk, or was it already priced into your thesis?**
 
Answered: no, it doesn't change the risk view — it removes one specific tail risk (an *accelerating* RL loss) without adding new information that the thesis didn't already assume. A durable-earnings-growth thesis for META has to already tolerate a large, sustained RL loss, since RL has lost money every year at increasing scale. "Similar to 2025" is the base case that thesis already implicitly required; it's a mild positive relative to the alternative (worsening losses) but not evidence the segment has become safer. Treat this as confirmation of an existing assumption, not a new tailwind.
 
**2. Does the scale of the aggregate legal exposure statement ("up to hundreds of billions of dollars" in maximum theoretical exposure across all matters) change your risk weighting, given it is a ceiling figure rather than an expected outcome?**
 
Answered: no, not on its own. A stated maximum aggregated across every pending matter, most of which are contested or under appeal, is definitionally an upper bound built for worst-case disclosure purposes, not a probability-weighted estimate — companies of META's size and litigation surface routinely carry ceiling language like this without it representing the expected loss. What *would* change the risk weighting is a pattern in the itemized, non-ceiling figures: several specific fines/settlements in the hundreds-of-millions-to-low-billions range, recurring on a roughly annual cadence (2020 FTC $5.0B, 2023 privacy $725M, 2024-2025 EU fines in the hundreds of millions each). That recurring pattern is the more decision-relevant signal than the ceiling number, and it's already priced into "regulatory exposure exists and is ongoing" as a known, bounded cost of doing business rather than a discrete risk to react to.
 
## Decision
 
**Decision: Hold / Watch.**
 
**Why:** The operating evidence remains strong on its own terms — FoA operating income +18%, ad pricing and impressions both growing, free cash flow still ~$43.6B after very heavy capex. That part of the thesis is intact and doesn't require revisiting.
 
What changed is the valuation question, and it changed in a way that argues for *more* caution, not less. The forward P/E is no longer an unknown — it's 19.37, up from ~17 a week earlier — and the evidence audit is explicit that the "sentiment rally, not an earnings re-rating" explanation for that move is the contract's own narrative, not something independently confirmed against a consensus-EPS history. I'm treating that explanation as plausible (a single-week jump driven by product-launch and AI-monetization news is a more parsimonious explanation than a comparable one-week jump in consensus 2026-2027 EPS estimates), but plausible is not the same as verified, and the audit correctly declined to let it pass as settled.
 
That matters for the decision itself: even at the low end of the range checked (~17), the multiple wasn't obviously cheap against the operating evidence; at 19.37, on a rally that the evidence doesn't yet show is earnings-backed, the valuation case is weaker than it was two weeks ago, not stronger. A multiple expanding faster than the underlying earnings estimate is, by definition, the stock getting more expensive per dollar of forward earnings — that's true regardless of whether the cause was sentiment or fundamentals, and it's the opposite of what would justify moving off Hold toward a more constructive stance.
 
So the call stays Hold/Watch, but the reasoning shifts: it's no longer "waiting on a missing number," it's "the number arrived, moved in the less favorable direction, and its cause is still unconfirmed." Two threshold conditions would revisit this: (a) a consensus-EPS-estimate check showing the 2026/2027 forward-earnings numbers actually moved up alongside the price, which would mean the multiple expansion is earnings-backed rather than pure sentiment, or (b) the multiple compressing back toward the ~17 area on a subsequent pull, which would remove the "getting more expensive" concern. Absent either, there's no basis in this evidence to upgrade the stance, and the operating strength alone isn't a reason to chase a multiple that just expanded on an unconfirmed narrative.
