## Causal claims vs. correlation

- **None of the metrics above assert a causal story.** The card's language ("driven by advertising revenue," capex "against" operating income) is descriptive/comparative, not a causal claim that AI capex *caused* the operating-income increase. Flagging this explicitly because it's the easiest overreach to slide into: FoA operating income rising 18% alongside heavy AI-related capex is consistent with a capex-pays-off story, but the evidence here does not isolate causation — it is two trends reported in the same filing. **NEEDS HUMAN**: if the card is ever revised to say AI spending "is driving" the income growth, that crosses from "can suggest" to an unsupported causal claim.
- **New this pass:** the forward-P/E contract attributes the jump from ~17 to 19.37 to a price rally on AI-monetization enthusiasm outrunning earnings estimates, rather than to a change in consensus EPS. That attribution is plausible and stated as such in the contract, but it has not been independently verified here against a consensus-EPS-estimate time series — it is the contract's own narrative gloss on the number, not a decomposed price-vs-estimate calculation. **NEEDS HUMAN** if this attribution is going to bear weight in the decision (e.g., "the multiple expanded on sentiment, not fundamentals") rather than just being read as color.
