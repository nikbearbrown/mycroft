## Notes

- **The first run crashed** at filing 123 of 593: a model answer ran past its 300-token cap and came back as unterminated JSON. Nothing from that run was saved or scored. Fix (in `triage.py`, before any scoring): an answer that does not parse keeps the filing, flagged — triage is a recall-first filter. Then a full rerun.
- **The Week 3 report found a Week 2 defect (P40).** Its first version showed 21 of 22 Week 2 announcements kept; the one 'dropped' was a Core Scientific earnings release that `explain_changes.py` had credited with the 0% 2031 convertibles because "0%" matched inside "escalates 20%" near "Nov 2031" in a lease table. The coupon must now start a number; the notes are credited to the December 2024 8-K that announced them (kept by rule R1).
- **Seen while labelling Week 3's set, fixed in Week 4**: an 8-K printing `Item1.01` without a space defeated `_strip_boilerplate`; a preliminary 424B2 whose cover leaves the coupon and year blank (`% Senior Notes due 20`) escaped rule R2 and was kept by R4 for the wrong reason. Because those fixes came from reading Week 3's set, that set is a calibration set from Week 4 on, and Week 4 drew a fresh one.
- **Week 4's fresh set was labelled before Week 4's run**; the labels file's hash is recorded in `manifests/week4_gate.json`.
- **The week 4 check does not pass (8 of 9).** The miss is IBM's preliminary euro prospectus (Feb 2025): its cover leaves even the century of the year blank (`€ % Notes due`), so the Week 4 blank-cover pattern (`% Notes due 20`) does not fire, and rule R2 then finds "Preferred Stock" in the cover's table of contents and calls it an equity offering. Recorded as P46 and **not fixed against this set** — a fix found here would make it a calibration set. It goes to Week 5 with a third fresh check. The Week 3 pattern this resembles was fixed for one blank form and not for its neighbour.
- **One document is in both labelled sets.** Dell's October 2024 pricing term sheet was filed twice, under each co-issuer's CIK, with different accession numbers; the timeline removes co-filings by accession, so both copies are in the pool, and the fresh draw picked the second copy. Recorded as P45 (a timeline defect) and left in the set as drawn.
- **The false alarms, and one probable labelling error.** Three are borderline by the labels' own convention: an earnings release recapping a note conversion (rule R4), a fee-table refiling for a share offering that names the concurrent notes (rule R2), and a warrant issued for a loan announced earlier (model). The fourth is probably the label's fault: Applied Digital's January 2025 earnings release says a subsidiary *closed* a $150 m senior secured financing with Macquarie, and the label (written from the release's lead and three debt-word passages) does not mention it. Labels are not changed after the run, so it stays a false alarm here; counted as relevant, precision would be 9 of 12 and recall 9 of 10.
- **Week 5: P46 and P45 fixed, then a third fresh set.** The blank-cover pattern now takes `% Notes due` with anything after it, and a base prospectus's contents list ("Description of Preferred Stock") no longer counts as a share sale. Co-filed copies of one document are one filing (same day, form, file name and text). Week 4's set, where P46 was found, is a calibration from now on (9 of 9 above).
- **Week 5's fresh set was labelled before Week 5's run** (git blob `9d88931006c11346804e4cdbb16a3095d6ce7b4d`, unchanged after the run; `manifests/week5_gate.json`). Week 4's run over the same pool existed but was not read while labelling.
- **The check passes: 15 of 15.** It is still small: one miss would have been 93%, below the line.
- **Three false alarms, two of them new rule weaknesses (logged, not fixed against this set):** rule R4's tender pattern fired on a third party's *mini-tender for Alphabet's shares* (P53: a tender for shares is not a debt tender); rule R3 kept Cipher Mining's FWP for a borrowed-share offering because it names the concurrent convertible notes (P54: an FWP offering shares is not a note term sheet; Week 4's labels treat that as not relevant). The third is the model's: Intel's sale of Altera, where the debt commitment letter is the *buyer's*.

The event reader does not use triage's event type, so this costs nothing downstream yet.

## Who decided what

Rule decisions are code. Model decisions are **model judgments** (P8), flagged `model_judgment: true` in every record, with a quoted piece of evidence checked against the text. The labels are Claude's reading of each filing against the definition (a model judgment too, made before triage was run on that set, by a different model than the one under test).
