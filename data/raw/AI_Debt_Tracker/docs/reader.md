## What the numbers say

- **The check passes, and B2 is better at numbers.** The one-call baseline B2 — the model typing each value as a number — read 160 of 160 fields on this set (and 130 of 132 on the development documents). The reader read 157 of 167. On pricing documents like these, a 4B model reading numbers is already reliable; what the reader adds is not accuracy but **provenance**: every value it reports was found in a cited passage and parsed by code, and a value the model 'copied' but no passage contains is refused. None of B2's numbers is tied to a sentence.
- **What the refusal caught.** Of the 24 dropped copies, **8 are currency signs the model rewrote while 'copying'**: "$1,500,000,000" where Alphabet's 8-K says "€1,500,000,000" (five tranches), Digital Realty's €850 m and Equinix's €600 m likewise, and Equinix's CHF 100 m as "$100 million". Refused, those principals are missing rather than wrong. The prompt's own example amount is in dollars; that is the likely pull. The other **16 are the word "null" written as text** for a value the filing does not state — a bookkeeping bug (the schema allows a string), with no effect on any score, since the field is empty either way; fixed in code for the next run.
- **Three reader defects, recorded and not fixed against this set** (fixing them here would turn it into a calibration set): (1) the currency sign rewritten when copying; (2) passage selection dropped Super Micro's "$500 million term loan facility" sentence — an amount and a debt word but no rate or year scores below the threshold — so that filing had no passages at all; (3) on a preliminary prospectus with every term blank (Equinix, Aug 2024) the model listed the cover's table of *existing* notes: 15 of the reader's 29 extras.
- **Development, before the evaluation set was touched**: three rounds on the 25 bake-off documents not in this set. Round 1: 95% of fields, 25 extras. Round 2 (prompt: what not to list, what each action means; code: a tranche repeated without its amount is merged): 9 extras, but two long prospectuses ran past the output cap. Round 3 (code: a cut-off answer keeps the instruments it completed, flagged): 96%, 10 extras. Then frozen and run here once.
- **Small and easy.** 24 filings, most of them pricing documents of large investment-grade issuers; 62 of the 66 instruments are new notes. Loans, amendments and redemptions — where reading is harder — are 4. EX-4/EX-10 exhibits are not read yet (Applied Digital's note terms live only there).
- **Who decided what.** Which instruments a filing announces, and each one's action, are model judgments (P8), flagged in every event. The truth is Claude's reading, checked by machine only for new bonds' coupon, year and principal against the next report.
